Your all-staff reference for understanding and implementing MTSS at CRES. Use it to find the right supports for every student at every level.

2026–27 School Year

How to Use This Playbook

MTSS at CRES is a school-wide system of support for every student. Every student receives Core Instruction (Tier 1). Some students receive Supplemental Instruction (Tier 2) in addition to Core Instruction (Tier 1). A few students receive Intensive Instruction (Tier 3) in addition to Core Instruction and Supplemental Instruction. Use this playbook to understand what each tier looks like and how to respond when a student needs more.

Core Instruction (Tier 1) (All students)

Strong, standards-aligned instruction with scaffolds, formative assessment, and positive classroom systems. Every student receives this every day. Start here first.

See Literacy, Math, or Behavior tabs for Core Instruction (Tier 1) expectations.

Supplemental Instruction (Tier 2) (Some students)

Targeted small-group support for students not responding to Core Instruction alone. Provided in addition to core instruction, never instead of it.

See pathway tabs for entry criteria, resources, and structure.

Intensive Instruction (Tier 3) (Few students)

Highly targeted, individualized support for students with significant and persistent needs. Involves the Intervention Team and increased frequency.

See pathway tabs and Flowcharts for Intensive Instruction (Tier 3) guidance.

Core + More: Tiers are additive and layered. A student in Intensive Instruction (Tier 3) still receives Core Instruction instruction every day. Intervention is never a replacement for grade-level access.

  • NCEnsi, DreamBox

Pathway Workflows

Literacy

  • Review mCLASS, classroom assessments, student work, and instructional patterns.
  • Use the 80% rule to determine whether this is a Core Instruction (Tier 1) concern.
  • Use diagnostic review to identify the Specific Area of Need.
  • Match intervention to the skill need and monitor progress.

Math

  • Review NKT, STAR Math, AMC, DreamBox, NCCI, classroom assessments, and student work.
  • Use the 80% rule to review Core Instruction (Tier 1) math instruction first.
  • Identify the specific prerequisite or grade-level skill need.
  • Match intervention and monitor progress over 3–4 data points.

Behavior

  • Begin with PBIS/ROAR expectations, routines, relationships, and reinforcement.
  • Review behavior data, patterns, setting, frequency, and intensity.
  • Identify the replacement behavior that needs to be taught.
  • Match support and monitor student response over time.

Attendance

  • Review attendance patterns, absences, tardies, and missed instruction.
  • Begin with family outreach and barrier review.
  • Use Supplemental or Intensive Instruction attendance thresholds to determine next steps.
  • Monitor attendance trends and document outreach/support.

Before Changing the Plan

Before increasing support, changing intervention, or moving to another tier, teams should pause and make sure the current plan had a fair opportunity to work.

Ask these questions first:
  • Was the support provided as planned?
  • Did the student receive the full amount of support?
  • Was attendance consistent enough for the support to work?
  • Was the support matched to the student’s specific need?
  • Do we have enough progress-monitoring data to make a decision?
  • Have we discussed the student’s response during PLC, Kid Talk, or the Intervention Team?

At Creech Road, We Aim To Build an Optimizing MTSS System

An optimizing MTSS system is not about adding more paperwork. It means our school uses clear processes, shared language, strong instruction, consistent progress monitoring, and team-based problem solving so students receive the right support at the right time.

1. Leadership

We aim to: keep MTSS connected to our school vision, improvement plan, staffing, resources, and daily decisions.

  • Leaders actively support MTSS implementation.
  • Teams have clear roles and meeting structures.
  • School-wide needs are reviewed and addressed with data.

2. Capacity & Infrastructure

We aim to: make sure staff have the time, tools, training, schedules, and support needed to implement MTSS well.

  • Staff understand assessments, data sources, and decision rules.
  • Schedules allow time for intervention, progress monitoring, and problem solving.
  • Resources are matched to student need.

3. Communication & Collaboration

We aim to: keep staff, families, students, and support teams informed and involved.

  • Families are partners in problem solving.
  • Teams communicate across PLCs, Kid Talk, Intervention Team, and ILT.
  • Staff receive information about student outcomes and MTSS implementation.

4. Data Based Problem Solving (DBPS)

We aim to: use data to identify the concern, understand why it is happening, create a plan, and evaluate response.

  • Teams define the gap between current and expected performance.
  • Teams analyze instruction, curriculum, environment, and learner factors.
  • Plans include what will happen, who will do it, when it will happen, and how progress will be reviewed.

5. Multiple Levels of Support

We aim to: provide Core Instruction, Supplemental Instruction, and Intensive Instruction that are clearly defined and matched to need.

  • Core Instruction is strengthened before assuming the concern is individual.
  • Supplemental Instruction is matched to the specific skill or behavior need.
  • Intensive Instruction increases support when needs are significant and persistent.

6. Data & Evaluation

We aim to: use assessment, progress monitoring, and implementation data to know what is working and what needs to change.

  • Universal screening helps identify risk and review Core Instruction.
  • Progress monitoring shows whether students are responding to support.
  • Teams use data to refine instruction, intervention, schedules, and resources.
What this means for staff: MTSS is not a separate program. It is the way we use instruction, assessment, collaboration, and student support systems to ensure every student can access learning and make progress.

PLC, Kid Talk, and Data Based Problem Solving (DBPS)

The problem-solving process begins with the grade-level team during PLC meetings. Teams use the Data to Action Protocol to collect and analyze data, make plans, and monitor impact. Departments may be invited by the grade-level chair, instructional coach, or ITF to attend PLC and/or Kid Talk meetings when data indicates a need.

Decision QuestionAction
Are fewer than 80% of students or subgroups meeting benchmark?Review and strengthen Core Instruction, curriculum, environment, and assessment.
Does the student have a repeated concern confirmed by multiple data points?Identify the Specific Area of Need (SAN) and consider Supplemental Instruction support.
Does the team have 3–4 intervention data points showing limited response?Bring to Kid Talk and use ICEL/RIOT to adjust the plan.
Is the student still not responding after targeted support?Review fidelity, intensity, match to need, attendance, and learner factors; consider Intervention Team support.

ICEL / RIOT Framework

ICEL/RIOT is a problem-solving framework that helps teams avoid assuming the concern is automatically within the learner. Teams review Instruction, Curriculum, and Environment first before Learner factors.

ICEL AreaWhat Teams Check
Instruction (I)How the curriculum is taught: explicit instruction, pacing, clarity, modeling, feedback, engagement, grouping, and fidelity.
Curriculum (C)What is taught: standards alignment, materials, scope/sequence, difficulty, format, relevance, and prerequisite skills.
Environment (E)Where learning occurs: routines, expectations, safety, seating, peer context, management, attendance, family/community factors.
Learner (L)Who is being taught: prior knowledge, skills, motivation, health/vision/hearing, attendance, behavior history, response to intervention.
RIOT SourceExamples
Review (R)Records, student work, assessments, attendance, discipline, intervention logs, progress monitoring.
Interview (I)Teacher, student, family, counselor, social worker, interventionist, support staff.
Observe (O)Instruction, transitions, engagement, peer interactions, behavior patterns, task access, time on task.
Test (T)Screeners, diagnostics, probes, inventories, surveys, rating scales, FBA-related data, progress monitoring.

WCPSS Literacy Decision Rules

80% Benchmark Rule

Ask: Are 80% of students meeting benchmark? Are 80% of each subgroup?

If no: Strengthen Core Instruction (Tier 1) with scaffolds, differentiation, and review of instruction, curriculum, environment, and assessment before identifying as an individual need.

Diagnostic Assessment & SAN

Students needing more than core support may require diagnostic assessment to determine why the gap is occurring and identify the Specific Area of Need (SAN).

75% Intervention Rule

Ask: Are 75% of students in the intervention group responding? Is the individual student responding?

If no: Review fidelity, focus, intensity, and match to SAN. If yes: Target next skills or plan gradual release to core.

Core + More: Literacy intervention is layered on top of Core Instruction instruction. Intervention does not replace access to grade-level core instruction.
Core Instruction (Tier 1) Literacy

What every student receives

  • Standards-aligned core literacy instruction.
  • Learning targets posted and unpacked with students.
  • Vocabulary pre-teaching, word banks, sentence stems, graphic organizers, and comprehension scaffolds.
  • Small-group literacy instruction and checks for understanding.
  • Access to grade-level content with temporary scaffolds.

Decision rule: If fewer than 80% of students or subgroups meet literacy benchmark, strengthen Core Instruction (Tier 1) before identifying the concern as an individual intervention need.

Supplemental Instruction (Tier 2) Literacy

Entry criteria

  • Below benchmark or repeated skill gap confirmed by multiple data sources.
  • Specific Area of Need identified through diagnostic review.
  • Student needs targeted support in addition to differentiated core.

Resources

  • K–2: Letterland Intervention.
  • 3–5: Recipe for Reading.
  • K–5 Literacy Intervention Guidance.

Structure

2–5 days per week · 45–60 minutes per week · no more than 8 students.

Response rule

If fewer than 75% of the intervention group are responding AND/OR the individual student is not responding — review fidelity, focus, attendance, time, group size, and match to SAN.

Intensive Instruction (Tier 3) Literacy

Entry criteria

  • Significant and persistent literacy need.
  • Limited response to matched Supplemental Instruction support after data review and adjustments.
  • Need requires Intervention Team review and increased intensity.

Resources

  • K–2: Letterland Intervention Strand.
  • 3–5: Recipe for Reading.
  • Individualized intensive literacy plan aligned to SAN, documented in ECATS.

Structure

4–5 days per week · 61–150 minutes per week · no more than 3 students.

Appendix A: Core Instruction (Tier 1) Scaffolding

Scaffolding provides temporary, intentional supports that help all students access grade-level standards while maintaining rigor. Planned during lesson design and removed as students gain independence.

What Scaffolding IS

  • Access to grade-level content
  • Temporary — removed as mastery increases
  • Proactively planned in lesson design
  • Aligned to cognitive demand
  • Part of core instruction

What Scaffolding Is NOT

  • Not Supplemental Instruction or Intensive Instruction intervention
  • Not lowering expectations
  • Not giving answers
  • Not permanent
Scaffold TypeExamples
Content AccessPre-teaching vocabulary, anchor charts, graphic organizers, sentence stems and frames, chunking text/tasks.
Cognitive ProcessingThink-alouds, guided notes, worked examples, checklists and rubrics.
Engagement & AccessMultimodal instruction, strategic partnering, flexible output options.
Executive FunctionModeled steps, timers and pacing tools, clear routines and procedures.

Core Instruction (Tier 1) Behavior — PBIS
  • PBIS / ROAR expectations explicitly taught, modeled, practiced, reinforced, and retaught.
  • Second Step SEL instruction.
  • Morning Meeting and relationship-building routines.
  • Positive reinforcement systems.
  • Restorative practices and classroom management systems.

Before moving to Supplemental Instruction (Tier 2): Review PBIS expectation instruction, reinforcement frequency, routines, relationships, attendance, and access to instruction.

Decision rule: If fewer than 80% of students or subgroups are meeting behavior goals, review and strengthen Core Instruction (Tier 1) before identifying as an individual intervention need.

Supplemental Instruction (Tier 2) Behavior — Supplemental

Entry criteria

  • More than 2 In School Suspension (ISS) / Out of School Suspension (OSS) or more than 3 repeated incidents per quarter.
  • 5 or more unexcused absences.
  • Inadequate response to school-wide behavior expectations.
  • Behaviors resulting in loss of instructional time or physical/emotional harm; referrals documented in ECATS.

Resources

  • CICO (Check-In Check-Out).
  • Mentor.
  • Referral to SISP for individual or small-group sessions.
  • Supplemental Behavior Plan.
  • Lunch Detention / Tiger Turn Around.
  • Parent conference (face-to-face or virtual).

Progress monitoring

Class Dojo data tools, behavior charts, referral patterns, and goal tracking. The designee to teach desired/replacement behavior will be determined at the PTR meeting.

Intensive Instruction (Tier 3) Behavior — Intensive

Entry criteria

  • Plan moves to intensive based on data and adjustments to current interventions.
  • Student is not meeting 80% of behavior goals on Supplemental Plan.
  • Behavior results in loss of instructional time evidenced by charts, attendance, In School Suspension (ISS), or Out of School Suspension (OSS).

Resources

  • PTR: Prevent, Teach, Reinforce plan made by the team.
  • FBA-related data collection.
  • Individualized behavior support with 3 or fewer students.
  • Parent conference with administrators and teachers.
  • Restorative actions in Tiger Turnaround and administrative consequences when appropriate.

Notes

The designee to teach desired/replacement behavior and environment decisions will be determined at the PTR team meeting.

Attendance Pathway

Use this section when absences, tardies, or missed instruction may be impacting progress. Chronic absence means missing 10% or more of school days for any reason — excused, unexcused, or suspended.

TierTriggerSupportWho Helps

Intensive Instruction (Tier 3)10%+ missed days AND history of absenteeismSocial worker, home visit, community resources, court referral when appropriateSocial Worker, admin, attendance team

At Creech Road, We Aim To...

  • Identify attendance concerns early.
  • Partner with families to understand barriers.
  • Use attendance data to determine the level of support needed.
  • Monitor attendance trends and outreach.
  • Connect students and families to supports when barriers persist.

Before Changing the Plan

Before increasing support, changing intervention, or moving tiers, pause and check:

  • Has the family been contacted and the barrier explored?
  • Are absences excused, unexcused, or part of a pattern?
  • Has the team reviewed the student’s attendance percentage and missed instruction?
  • Have school-based supports or community resources been considered?
  • Is the current support improving attendance trends?

Core Instruction (Tier 1) Attendance
  • Track and record excused and unexcused absences per WCPSS policy.
  • Create a school climate that encourages daily attendance.
  • Use School Messenger and personalized family outreach.
  • Discuss attendance trends in PLCs.
  • After 3 unexcused absences: Teacher contacts parent/guardian.
  • After 6 unexcused absences: Data Manager sends 1700 mandatory attendance letter.
Supplemental Instruction (Tier 2) Attendance

Entry criteria: Student misses 5–9% of school days AND has a past history of missing school and/or a risk factor.

  • Caring-adult check-in to connect student with a supportive adult.
  • Data-based problem solving (EWS Indicators in ECATS).
  • Personalized family outreach.
  • Family/school conference (Principal, AP, Teacher, Counselor, Social Worker).
  • Connect to additional resources as needed (mental health, school nurse, etc.).
Intensive Instruction (Tier 3) Attendance

Entry criteria: Student misses 10% or more of school days AND has past absenteeism/risk factors.

  • Monitor attendance with multiple data points and circumstances.
  • Involve School Social Worker: determine good faith effort, conduct home visit when needed.
  • Monitor No Show process using Due Diligence Checklist when applicable.
  • Consider community resources or court referral when appropriate.
  • Explore alternative education options (modified schedules, before/after school wraparound).

Attendance Team Responsibilities

TierWCPSS-Aligned Attendance Actions
Core Instruction (Tier 1)Track excused and unexcused absences, review chronic absenteeism reports, create a climate that encourages daily attendance, build staff knowledge, and use School Messenger for personalized family communication.
Supplemental Instruction (Tier 2)For 5–9% missed days with history/risk factors: establish check-in with a caring adult, use Data Based Problem Solving (DBPS), provide personalized family outreach, schedule family/school conference, and connect to resources such as counselor, social worker, nurse, or mental health supports.

When to Consider a Special Education Evaluation

A special education referral may be appropriate when a student has received intensive, individualized Intensive Instruction (Tier 3) intervention with fidelity for 8–12 weeks and data continues to show insufficient progress.

Document before referring

All Supplemental Instruction and Intensive Instruction interventions, fidelity records, progress monitoring data, and all family communication logs.

MTSS data is evaluation data

mCLASS, NKT, STAR Math, ICEL/RIOT analysis, and intervention response data should all be included in the evaluation process.

Team decision

Dr. Daly (Special Education) and Mrs. Morton (Psychologist) lead the referral and evaluation discussion with the Intervention Team.

Important: MTSS is not a prerequisite for special education evaluation. If there is reason to suspect a disability at any time, a referral can be made regardless of tier placement.
